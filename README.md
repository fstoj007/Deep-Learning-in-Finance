# Deep-Learning-in-Finance

This repository contains a structured collection of lecture materials for a (hypothetical) course on deep 
learning in finance. Most of the material (lectures 1-10) is directly lifted from the materials prepared by 
Josef Teichmann for the course **“Mathematics for New Technologies in Finance”** at ETH Zurich. The material for 
lecutre 11 was provided by me, and as more material gets added and the scope of the course becomes more refined, 
the relative balance of contributions from myself and Teichmann will change. The attribution section will be 
kept updated accordingly.

## Overview

Some of the main (highly interrelated) problems one encounters in quantitative finance are
1. pricing financial instruments,
2. hedging one's position in an investment,
3. selecting a model describing the evolution of market assets given historical data.
Exact (or even approximate) solutions to the above problems are often computationally intractable,
especially in the high-dimensional settings we see in real-world markets. A major challenge in quantitative
finance is therefore to develop efficient ways of (approximately) solving the aforementioned problems
in increasingly more realistic settings.

It has been repeatedly observed across many fields that neural networks offer efficient and accurate 
means of approximating solutions to many high-dimensional functional equations and optimization problems, 
especially in settings where traditional numerical methods exhibit the so-called "curse of dimensionality". 
As such, there has been much interest in finance to leverage neural networks to solve the above central 
problems of pricing, hedging, and model calibration.

This course will provide theoretical insight into why neural networks are useful as "universal approximators"
and present algorithms that have recently been proposed to solve fairly general instances of the above
problems.
